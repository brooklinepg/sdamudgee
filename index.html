Welcome to The Post

Where everyday living feels genuinely yours.

The Post is a brand-new specialist disability accommodation development at 334 Peel Street, in the established western side of Bathurst. Two separate homes, each single storey, each with its own front door, garage and garden.

Both are built to High Physical Support — the highest SDA design category — with fire sprinklers throughout and properly landscaped gardens. Wide doorways and level floors are there because that's how the homes were drawn, not because rails were added afterwards.

It's built by Brookline, with housing managed by ADAPT Housing as the registered SDA provider.

Finding a place that genuinely suits you shouldn't take years. We'd love to show you around this one.

Where the name comes from

This corner has been looking after people for eighty years.

In September 1942, Bathurst was full of families who had come to work at the munitions factory out along the Blayney Road. They needed somewhere to post letters home and collect their pay, so a small building went up in the corner of this very block.

It was the Bathurst West Post Office, and a woman named Joyce Ada Bourke ran it. She was the first postmistress the district ever had. She and her husband William were raising five sons in the house out the front at the time.

That little building served the street for more than thirty years. It was small, it was plain, and it mattered enormously to the people around it.

We liked that idea so much we kept the name.

“A small separate building was established in the western corner of the allotment for the Bathurst West Post Office in September of 1942. Ada Bourke became the first post mistress.” Statement of Heritage Impact, 334 Peel Street

What is SDA

Housing that's funded for you.

Specialist Disability Accommodation is housing funded through the NDIS for people with extreme functional impairment or very high support needs.

Your rent is covered through your NDIS plan.

SDA funding is paid to the provider through your plan, which means eligible participants can live in purpose-built accessible housing without carrying standard rental costs. You get to focus on living well rather than on bills.

How the funding works

SDA pays for the building itself — the bricks and mortar. It's separate from the funding that pays for your day-to-day supports, which you arrange through your own support provider.

Am I eligible?

SDA is for NDIS participants with extreme functional impairment or very high support needs. Your support coordinator, planner or occupational therapist can help you check whether it's in your plan.

How do I apply?

Get in touch with us and we'll connect you with the right people — ADAPT Housing for the property and tenancy, and a support provider of your choosing for daily care. We're happy to help either way.

Questions

Everything people usually ask.

If your question isn't here, just ask us. Nothing is too small or too obvious.

Are these homes for sale, or to rent?

To rent. We're not selling them. Brookline owns the property and intends to keep owning it, because we'd rather be accountable to the people living here than to a buyer. Registering your interest puts you on the list for availability and how to apply.

What will it cost me?

If you have SDA funding in your NDIS plan, that funding covers the housing itself. Participants also make a reasonable rent contribution, which is set by the NDIS rather than by us. We'll walk you through exactly what that means for your situation, with no surprises.

What is SDA, and how do I know if I'm eligible?

Specialist Disability Accommodation is NDIS funding for the building itself, for people with very high support needs or extreme functional impairment. It's separate from the funding that pays for your day-to-day supports.

Whether you have it is decided through your NDIS plan. A support coordinator or occupational therapist can check for you, and ADAPT Housing can help arrange a housing solution report. Ask us and we'll point you in the right direction, whether or not you end up living here.

What does High Physical Support mean?

HPS is the highest of the four SDA design categories. It's for people with very high support needs, and the building has to meet a tougher standard: structural provision for ceiling hoists, reinforced walls, wider circulation space that works with a powered wheelchair, and bathrooms designed for fully assisted use.

In practice it means the home is ready for the support you need now and the support you might need later, without anyone opening up a wall down the track. Your NDIS plan needs to include HPS SDA funding for you to live here — your support coordinator or OT can confirm that.

What does the support worker's room actually mean?

It's an OOA room — overnight onsite assistance. A proper bedroom for a support worker who stays the night, with its own door, set apart from your bedroom. Someone is there if you need help at 3am. They're not sleeping on your couch or sharing your bathroom.

Can I keep my own support provider?

Yes. Your home and your supports are funded separately and chosen separately, so living at The Post doesn't tie you to anyone. Bring the people you already trust. If you're still looking for a provider, we're happy to point you towards ones working in Bathurst.

Can my family visit? Can I bring my own furniture?

Yes and yes. It's your home and your lease. Each home has its own front door, its own living rooms and its own garden. Visitors don't need anyone's permission, and you should absolutely make the place look like yours.

Can I come and see it?

Please do. Get in touch and we'll arrange a time to walk you through the plans and the site, and answer whatever you want to ask. Bring your family, your support coordinator, your OT — whoever helps you make decisions like this.

When will they be ready?

We're working through council approval at the moment. We won't give you a completion date we can't stand behind, so register your interest and we'll tell you the moment we know something firm.
